name: market-research
description: Conduct a structured market research analysis for a product, service, business idea, or market-entry question. Use this skill when asked to understand a market, customers, competitors, trends, opportunities, risks, or research gaps.
Market Research Skill
Purpose
Create a simple, structured, practical market research report that helps a business understand a market and make better decisions.
The analysis must connect:
Research Question → Market → Customer → Competition → Trends → Opportunities → Risks → Research Gaps → Recommendations
The output should be understandable to an MBA student and practical enough to use in a business discussion or assignment.
---
Inputs
Identify the available information about:
Product, service, or business idea
Industry or market
Geographic market
Target customer
Research objective or business question
Known competitors
Price or business model, if provided
Existing customer or market information
Research period, if relevant
Any specific information the user wants investigated
Not every input is mandatory.
If important information is missing:
Identify it.
Do not silently invent it.
Continue with a useful analysis when possible.
Clearly label assumptions, recommendations, and research gaps.
---
Core Process
Follow these steps in order.
Step 1 — Define the Research Question
Clearly state:
What is being researched?
Why is it being researched?
What business decision could the research support?
Do not change the user's main question without explaining why.
---
Step 2 — Understand the Market
Identify:
Market/industry
Geographic scope
Relevant customer segment
Product/service category
Market context
If market size or growth information is not available, say so.
Do not invent market statistics.
---
Step 3 — Understand the Customer
Analyze the customer information that is actually available.
Where possible, identify:
Customer segment
Customer need/problem
Buying situation
Possible decision factors
Possible barriers
Clearly distinguish:
Known information from research hypotheses.
Do not present assumed customer behavior as established fact.
---
Step 4 — Analyze Competition
Identify:
Direct competitors, if known
Indirect competitors or alternatives
Competitive categories
Known competitor positioning
Available points of differentiation
Do not invent:
Competitor revenue
Market share
Pricing
Features
Customer reviews
Customer perception
Performance
If competitor information is limited, clearly state the limitation.
---
Step 5 — Analyze Market Trends
Identify relevant trends that can affect the business.
For each trend, explain:
What the trend is
Why it may matter
Whether it is a verified fact, provided information, or strategic hypothesis
Do not create fake statistics or unsupported trend claims.
---
Step 6 — Identify Market Drivers and Barriers
Identify factors that may influence market development.
Examples:
Customer demand
Price sensitivity
Technology
Competition
Distribution
Regulation
Consumer behavior
Economic conditions
Operational constraints
Only include factors relevant to the specific case.
---
Step 7 — Identify Opportunities
Convert findings into potential business opportunities.
For every important opportunity, explain:
Opportunity
Evidence or reasoning
Potential business implication
Clearly label opportunities as recommendations, not facts.
---
Step 8 — Identify Risks
Identify important risks such as:
Strong competition
Unclear customer demand
Price pressure
Limited differentiation
Distribution challenges
Regulatory constraints
Missing information
Changing customer behavior
For each important risk, provide a practical mitigation or research action.
---
Step 9 — Identify Research Gaps
List information that is still required before making an important business decision.
Examples:
Customer survey data
Competitor pricing
Market size
Customer willingness to pay
Purchase frequency
Distribution information
Product preference
Brand awareness
Prioritize the research gaps:
High → Medium → Low
---
Step 10 — Develop Recommendations
Recommendations must follow:
Finding → Business Implication → Recommendation
Do not provide recommendations that are disconnected from the research.
Recommendations should be practical and specific.
---
Step 11 — Create an Executive Summary
Summarize:
Research question
Key market finding
Key customer finding
Competitive finding
Major opportunity
Major risk
Most important research gap
Recommended next action
---
Step 12 — Quality Check
Before presenting the final report, verify the Quality Check section.
---
Rules
Do not invent market data.
Do not invent customer research.
Do not invent competitor information.
Do not invent survey results.
Do not invent market size or growth rates.
Do not present assumptions as facts.
Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations.
Do not make unsupported claims about customers or competitors.
Do not create false sources or citations.
Keep the research question central to the analysis.
Keep recommendations connected to findings.
Prefer practical analysis over generic business language.
Clearly identify important information gaps.
Do not claim that the research proves something when the available information does not support it.
If current or external market information is required and no verified information is available, state that the information needs to be researched rather than inventing it.
---
Decision Rules
1. Missing Research Question
If the user has not provided a clear research question:
Ask for clarification if the analysis would otherwise be unreliable.
If the purpose is obvious from the context, state the assumed research question clearly.
Do not silently invent a major business objective.
---
2. Missing Market Information
If market information is missing:
Continue with the available information.
Clearly identify the gap.
Do not invent market size, growth, or industry statistics.
---
3. Missing Customer Information
If the customer is unclear:
Ask for clarification when necessary.
Otherwise describe the customer information that is known.
Recommend customer research where appropriate.
Do not create a detailed customer profile without evidence.
---
4. Missing Competitor Information
If competitors are not provided:
Identify the competitive category or alternatives if known.
State that specific competitor analysis requires additional information.
Do not invent competitor names or data.
---
5. Fact vs Assumption
Use these labels:
Provided Fact  
Information supplied by the user or supported by reliable evidence.
Assumption  
Information temporarily used because something is missing.
Hypothesis  
A proposition that should be tested through research.
Recommendation  
A strategic suggestion based on the analysis.
---
6. Conflicting Information
If information conflicts:
Identify the conflict.
Do not silently choose one version.
Ask for clarification when the conflict materially affects the analysis.
If a temporary assumption is necessary, clearly label it.
---
7. External Research
If the task requires current market facts, statistics, competitor information, regulations, prices, or trends that are not supplied:
Identify what needs verification.
Do not fabricate the information.
If reliable external research is available, distinguish researched information from user-provided information.
---
8. Recommendation Strength
The strength of a recommendation must reflect the strength of the evidence.
Strong evidence → stronger recommendation.
Limited evidence → cautious recommendation and/or further research.

Quality Check
Before presenting the final report, verify:
Research Question
Is the research question clear?
Does the analysis answer the question?
Market
Were market facts invented?
Were unsupported market statistics used?
Customer
Is customer information based on evidence?
Are hypotheses clearly labelled?
Competition
Were competitor facts invented?
Are competitive limitations visible?
Trends
Are trends supported?
Are trend-based assumptions clearly identified?
Opportunities
Are opportunities derived from findings?
Are they clearly labelled as recommendations?
Risks
Are important uncertainties identified?
Is mitigation practical?
Research Gaps
Are important unknowns listed?
Are high-priority gaps identified?
Recommendations
Does every major recommendation connect to a finding?
Is recommendation strength appropriate to the evidence?
Information Integrity
Are facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations separated?
Have unsupported claims been avoided?
Practicality
Can an MBA student or business team use the report to decide what to research or do next?
---
Final Principle
The research should follow:
Define → Understand → Analyze → Compare → Identify → Recommend → Validate
The AI should not:
Invent → Overclaim → Assume → Misrepresent
The purpose is not to produce a long research report.
The purpose is to produce a clear, evidence-aware, decision-useful market analysis.
