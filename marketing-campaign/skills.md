name: marketing-campaign
description: Create complete, structured, practical, and measurable marketing campaigns for product or service launches. Use this skill when asked to develop a campaign strategy, campaign concept, promotional plan, integrated campaign, content campaign, launch plan, media plan, or campaign execution plan.
Marketing Campaign Skill
Purpose
Create a complete marketing campaign plan for a product or service.
The campaign must connect:
Product → Audience → Market → Objective → Positioning → Message → Channels → Content → Budget → Timeline → CTA → KPIs → Optimization.
The output should be practical enough for a marketing team to use as a campaign planning document.
---
Inputs
Identify the available information about:
Product or service
Product type
Target audience
Geographic market
Campaign objective
Budget
Main competitors
Product positioning or differentiator
Campaign duration
Available marketing channels
Existing brand assets or digital presence
Distribution or purchase channels
Not every input is mandatory.
If important information is missing:
Identify it.
Do not silently invent it.
Continue with a useful draft when possible.
Clearly label assumptions and recommendations.
---
Core Process
Follow these steps in order.
Step 1 — Understand the Product
Identify:
What the product/service is
Product type
Known features
Known customer benefits
Price, if provided
Differentiator, if provided
Purchase/distribution information, if provided
Do not invent product features, benefits, ingredients, certifications, performance claims, or differentiators.
---
Step 2 — Understand the Target Audience
Identify:
Demographic information provided
Geographic information
Professional/customer segment
Stated needs or problems
Stated interests or behaviors
Do not invent audience characteristics and present them as facts.
If the audience is broad, recommend segmentation as a strategic recommendation rather than silently creating unsupported segments.
---
Step 3 — Understand the Market and Competition
Identify:
Market
Competitors
Competitive alternatives
Known positioning information
Available competitive advantages
If competitor information is limited, state the limitation.
Do not invent competitor pricing, features, market share, customer perception, or performance.
---
Step 4 — Define the Campaign Objective
Identify one primary objective unless the user explicitly requests multiple objectives.
Possible objectives include:
Brand awareness
Engagement
Lead generation
Product trial
Sales
Website traffic
App downloads
Customer retention
Translate the objective into measurable campaign outcomes where sufficient information is available.
---
Step 5 — Develop the Campaign Strategy
Create a logical strategy connecting:
Business Objective → Audience → Customer Need → Positioning → Message → Channel → Action → KPI
The strategy should explain why the campaign is being designed in this way.
Do not create strategy that is disconnected from the stated objective.
---
Step 6 — Develop the Campaign Concept
Create:
Campaign name
Campaign theme/concept
Core idea
Key message
Supporting messages, if useful
Tone of communication
The concept must be consistent with the product information.
---
Step 7 — Define Content Strategy
Create practical content pillars.
Examples:
Product awareness
Educational content
Problem/need-based content
Product-use occasions
Social proof
Community participation
Brand story
Select only relevant pillars.
Then create specific content ideas under each pillar.
---
Step 8 — Select Marketing Channels
Select channels based on:
Target audience
Campaign objective
Product/service
Budget
Campaign duration
Available resources
Possible channels include:
Instagram
Facebook
LinkedIn
YouTube
Search advertising
Email
Website/landing page
Influencer marketing
Offline marketing
Events
Partnerships
Sampling
Do not recommend channels simply because they are popular.
Explain the role of each selected channel.
---
Step 9 — Create the Budget Plan
If a budget is provided:
Keep the complete recommendation within the stated budget.
Provide a proposed allocation.
Distinguish recommended allocation from actual market cost.
Do not claim that a specific media cost is guaranteed.
A useful allocation may include:
Paid media
Content/creative production
Influencer/creator activity
Sampling/events/partnerships
Testing and optimization
The allocation is a strategic recommendation, not a statement of actual vendor pricing.
If the budget is not provided:
Do not invent a budget.
Provide a percentage-based framework or state that budget allocation requires additional information.
---
Step 10 — Create the Campaign Timeline
If campaign duration is provided:
Create a phased timeline such as:
Preparation
Launch
Awareness/engagement
Optimization
Campaign close/review
If campaign duration is not provided:
State that it is missing.
Recommend a reasonable planning duration only as an explicit recommendation.
Do not present the recommended duration as user-provided information.
---
Step 11 — Create an Execution Plan
Translate the strategy into actions.
Include, where relevant:
Activity
Channel
Owner/role
Frequency
Timeline
Required asset
KPI
Do not invent named employees or specific team members.
Use role names such as:
Marketing Manager
Content Executive
Designer
Performance Marketer
Social Media Manager
when useful.
---
Step 12 — Define the Call to Action
Create a CTA appropriate to the primary objective.
Examples:
Discover More
Learn More
Visit Website
Sign Up
Try Now
Book a Demo
Buy Now
Register
Share Your Experience
Do not automatically use "Buy Now."
---
Step 13 — Define KPIs
Select KPIs that directly correspond to the campaign objective.
For awareness:
Reach
Impressions
Video views
Video completion rate
Brand search volume, if measurable
For engagement:
Engagement rate
Comments
Shares
Saves
Participation rate
For lead generation:
Leads
Cost per lead
Landing-page conversion rate
For sales:
Revenue
Conversions
Conversion rate
Customer acquisition cost
ROAS
Define primary and secondary KPIs when useful.
Do not use vanity metrics as primary KPIs when they do not reflect the objective.
---
Step 14 — Define Measurement and Reporting
Create a simple measurement framework:
KPI
Measurement method
Reporting frequency
Decision/action based on the result
Examples of reporting frequency:
Daily for paid-media monitoring
Weekly for campaign performance
End-of-campaign for final evaluation
Only recommend a reporting frequency appropriate to the campaign.
---
Step 15 — Define Optimization
Explain what the marketing team should do when performance is:
Above expectation
On target
Below expectation
Examples:
Shift budget toward better-performing channels
Test alternative creative
Test different messages
Adjust audience targeting
Reduce underperforming activities
Do not promise that an optimization will produce a specific result.
---
Step 16 — Identify Risks and Constraints
Identify relevant risks such as:
Missing product information
Unsupported claims
Budget limitations
Short campaign duration
Limited creative resources
Unclear audience
Measurement limitations
Channel dependency
For each important risk, provide a practical mitigation.
---
Step 17 — Quality Check
Validate the complete campaign using the Quality Check section before presenting it.
---
Rules
General Rules
Do not invent product features.
Do not invent customer information.
Do not make unsupported claims.
Do not present assumptions as facts.
Keep the campaign objective clear.
Keep recommendations relevant to the target audience.
Keep recommendations realistic for the available resources.
Ensure strategy, message, content, channels, CTA, and KPIs are aligned.
Keep the complete budget within the provided budget.
Distinguish strategic recommendations from factual information.
Avoid unnecessary marketing jargon.
Prefer practical recommendations over generic advice.
Do not fabricate market research, competitor data, pricing, media costs, or performance results.
Do not claim that a campaign will definitely achieve a particular result.
---
Decision Rules
1. Missing Information
If important information is missing:
Do not silently invent it.
Clearly identify it.
Ask for clarification when it prevents reliable execution.
If a useful campaign can still be created, continue and label assumptions/recommendations.
Do not stop the entire campaign merely because a non-critical input is missing.
---
2. Missing Campaign Objective
Do not automatically assume the objective is sales.
If necessary, ask the user to confirm the objective.
If the user has already supplied the objective, use it as the primary direction.
---
3. Missing Campaign Duration
If duration is missing:
Clearly state that it was not provided.
Provide a recommended planning duration only if useful.
Label it as a recommendation.
Do not treat it as a confirmed campaign duration.
---
4. Missing Budget
If budget is missing:
Do not invent a monetary budget.
Provide a percentage-based framework or explain what budget information is required.
---
5. Budget Allocation
If budget is provided:
Sum all recommended allocations to exactly the provided budget.
Clearly label the allocation as a proposed planning allocation.
Do not present allocations as actual media/vendor prices.
---
6. Conflicting Information
If two pieces of information conflict:
Identify the conflict.
Do not silently choose one.
Ask for clarification when it materially affects the campaign.
If a temporary assumption is necessary, clearly state which information is being used and why.
---
7. Unsupported Claims
If the user asks for a claim that is not supported:
Do not present it as an established fact.
Flag the claim.
Request supporting evidence where necessary.
Suggest factual wording that does not make the unsupported claim.
---
8. Target Audience
If clearly specified:
Use it as the primary audience.
If broad:
Do not invent detailed demographic characteristics.
Recommend segmentation as a strategic next step.
If multiple audiences are provided:
Identify the primary audience only if the user provides enough information.
Otherwise identify the need for prioritization.
---
9. Campaign Objective and KPI Alignment
KPIs must follow the campaign objective.
Example:
Awareness → Reach / Impressions / Views
Lead Generation → Leads / CPL / Conversion Rate
Sales → Revenue / Conversions / CAC / ROAS
Do not evaluate an awareness campaign primarily using sales metrics unless explicitly requested.
---
10. Recommendation vs Fact
Clearly distinguish:
Provided fact
Information directly supplied by the user.
Assumption
Information temporarily used because required information is missing.
Recommendation
An AI-generated strategic suggestion.
Never present an AI recommendation as though it came from the user.

Quality Check
Before presenting the final campaign, verify:
Product Accuracy
Is the campaign based on actual product information?
Were product features invented?
Were unsupported claims introduced?
Audience Accuracy
Is the audience based on provided information?
Were unsupported demographic assumptions introduced?
Strategy
Does the strategy logically support the objective?
Is the positioning supported by available information?
Are recommendations clearly distinguished from facts?
Channel Selection
Are selected channels relevant to the audience?
Do channels support the objective?
Are they realistic for the budget and duration?
Content
Do content pillars support the campaign?
Does every major content idea have a purpose?
Are ideas practical to execute?
Budget
Does the proposed allocation equal the total provided budget?
Is the allocation clearly presented as a recommendation?
Are unsupported media-cost claims avoided?
Timeline
Are activities sequenced logically?
Is the duration provided by the user or clearly labelled as a recommendation?
CTA
Does the CTA support the campaign objective?
KPIs
Are primary KPIs directly connected to the objective?
Are KPIs measurable?
Is the measurement method practical?
Optimization
Are there clear actions for different performance levels?
Are optimization recommendations realistic?
Information Integrity
Are assumptions identified?
Are missing details identified?
Are conflicts identified?
Are recommendations distinguished from facts?
Have unsupported claims been avoided?
Practicality
Can the campaign reasonably be executed?
Does it respect budget and resource constraints?
Does it provide enough detail for a marketing team to act on?
---
Final Principle
The campaign should be driven by the information provided by the user.
The AI should:
Organize → Analyze → Strategize → Recommend → Plan → Measure → Optimize → Validate
The AI should not:
Invent → Assume → Misrepresent → Overclaim
When information is unclear, missing, or conflicting, preserve the uncertainty and make it visible.
The goal is not merely to produce creative marketing ideas.
The goal is to produce a complete, logically connected, measurable, and executable marketing campaign plan.
