Financial Analysis & Investment Decision Skill
Purpose
Create a structured, practical financial analysis that helps an MBA Finance student understand financial performance and evaluate a business or investment decision.
The analysis should connect:
Financial Data → Calculations → Comparison → Interpretation → Risk → Decision Considerations
The output should be understandable to an MBA student while using appropriate finance terminology.
Inputs
Identify the available information about:
Company, project, or investment
Analysis objective
Financial period
Income Statement data
Balance Sheet data
Cash Flow data
Historical financial data, if available
Investment amount, if relevant
Expected future cash flows, if relevant
Discount rate / required return, if provided
Terminal value, if relevant
Tax rate, if relevant
Industry benchmarks, if provided
Any specific financial metrics requested
Not every input is mandatory.
If important information is missing:
Identify it.
Do not invent financial figures.
Continue with the analysis where possible.
Clearly label assumptions and information gaps.

Step 7 — Evaluate Investment Projects
When investment data is provided, calculate relevant measures.
Payback Period
Determine how long it takes to recover the initial investment.
ROI
Calculate when the relevant investment and return data are available.
NPV
Use:
NPV = Present Value of Future Cash Flows − Initial Investment
Use the provided discount rate.
IRR
Estimate the discount rate at which NPV equals zero when sufficient cash-flow data is available.
Do not invent a discount rate.

Liquidity risk

Cash-flow risk
Investment risk

Step 10 — Sensitivity and Scenario Analysis
When sufficient information is available, examine how results change under different assumptions.
Examples:
Revenue growth
Operating costs
Discount rate
Initial investment
Cash-flow assumptions
Clearly label scenarios as assumptions.
Do not present hypothetical scenarios as forecasts or facts.
Step 11 — Interpret the Results
Use:
Calculation → Meaning → Financial Implication
Example:
Calculation: Net profit margin decreased.
Meaning: The company retains less profit from each unit of revenue.
Financial Implication: Investigate whether costs, pricing, interest, or taxes are responsible before drawing a conclusion.

Step 14 — Quality Check
Complete the quality check defined below.
Rules
Do not invent financial figures.
Do not invent financial statements.
Do not invent historical performance.
Do not invent industry benchmarks.
Do not invent discount rates.
Do not invent cash flows.
Show formulas for important calculations.
Show calculation inputs clearly.
Distinguish calculations from interpretation.
Distinguish facts from assumptions.
Do not assume the cause of a financial change without evidence.
Do not use irrelevant ratios.
Do not rely on a single financial metric when multiple relevant metrics are available.
Do not present scenarios as actual results.
Do not create false sources or citations.
If required data is missing, identify it rather than estimating silently.
Use finance terminology accurately.
Keep recommendations connected to the financial evidence.
State important limitations.
If external financial information is required and verified information is unavailable, clearly identify what needs to be researched.
Decision Rules
1. Missing Financial Data
If a calculation cannot be performed:
State the missing input.
Provide the formula where useful.
Do not invent the value.
2. Negative or Zero Denominator
If a ratio has a zero or unsuitable denominator:
Do not calculate a misleading ratio.
Explain why the ratio is not meaningful or cannot be calculated.
3. Multiple Years
When historical data is available:
Compare periods.
Highlight meaningful changes.
Avoid assuming causation.
4. Industry Benchmark
Only compare against an industry benchmark when the benchmark is provided or reliably verified.
Otherwise state:
“Industry benchmark not provided; comparison requires additional research.”
5. Investment Decision
Use the measures relevant to the decision.
For example:
NPV focuses on value creation at a given discount rate.
IRR expresses the project's implied rate of return.
Payback focuses on recovery period and does not by itself measure total value creation.
Do not declare a project attractive based on one measure alone without considering the decision context.
6. Discount Rate
Use the discount rate provided by the user.
If no discount rate is provided and NPV is required:
State that NPV cannot be finalized without a discount rate.
If appropriate, perform sensitivity analysis using clearly labelled illustrative assumptions rather than presenting one assumed rate as fact.
7. Conflicting Data
If financial inputs conflict:
Identify the conflict.
Do not silently choose one.
Ask for clarification when material.
If continuing is necessary, state the temporary assumption.
8. External Research
When current company financials, market data, benchmark data, interest rates, or other external information are required:
Identify what requires verification.
Use reliable external information when available.
Clearly distinguish external information from user-provided data and analysis.
Never fabricate missing information.
9. Recommendations
Strong evidence → stronger recommendation.
Limited evidence → cautious recommendation and/or additional analysis.

Final Principle
Collect → Calculate → Compare → Interpret → Assess Risk → Decide → Validate
Do not:
Invent → Miscalculate → Assume → Overclaim
The goal is not simply to produce financial ratios or investment metrics. The goal is to help an MBA Finance student understand what the numbers mean, why they matter, what risks exist, and what additional information is needed for a sound financial decision.
